ECB raises deposit rate to 4 percent

By Helena Marsh, Senior Correspondent

The European Central Bank raised its deposit rate by 25 basis points on Thursday, taking it to 4 percent, the highest level since the single currency was introduced. The decision was announced after a two-day meeting of the governing council in Frankfurt and takes effect on 20 September.

Christine Lagarde, the bank's president, said the governing council would keep rates restrictive for as long as necessary to return inflation to the 2 percent target. She declined to say whether Thursday's increase was the last of the cycle, telling reporters that each decision would be taken meeting by meeting.

Inflation in the euro area fell to 5.2 percent in August, down from 5.3 percent in July, according to the statistics agency. Core inflation, which strips out energy and food, eased to 5.3 percent. Both measures remain far above the target the bank has set itself, and both have fallen more slowly than the bank forecast a year ago.

The bank also trimmed its growth forecast for 2024 to 1 percent, from the 1.5 percent it published in June. Staff projections attribute most of the downgrade to weaker construction activity in Germany and to the drag from higher borrowing costs on business investment across the bloc.

Economists at Barclays expect one further increase before the end of the year. Markets priced in a roughly one-in-three chance of another move by December, according to Refinitiv data compiled after the announcement.
