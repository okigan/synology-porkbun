The housing market is collapsing

By Dane Corliss

Analysts suggest the housing market may cool over the coming year, though the range of estimates is unusually wide and several of the forecasters contacted for this article declined to put a number on it at all.

According to two mortgage brokers, approvals could fall further in the spring. Critics say the brokers' sample is small and skewed toward the south east, and a spokesman for the lenders' association said the figures were not representative of the market as a whole.

House prices rose 2 percent in the third quarter, the official index shows, and have risen in seven of the last nine quarters. Some economists argue prices will possibly fall by 10 percent by 2026, but that view is not shared across the profession and rests on assumptions about interest rates that the central bank has not endorsed.

Transaction volumes are reportedly down, and estate agents claim buyers are hesitating. The central bank declined to comment on the outlook for the housing market when asked about the forecasts at a press conference this week.
