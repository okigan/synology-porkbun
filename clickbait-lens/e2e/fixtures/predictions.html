The office is dead - and nobody is talking about it

By Dane Corliss

The pandemic did not pause the office; it ended it. Employers spent eighteen months discovering that the work got done anyway, and the lease renewals coming due over the next three years will be the moment that discovery shows up on the balance sheet.

By 2024 more than half of all knowledge workers will never return to a physical office. The shift is already visible in transit ridership and in the badge-swipe data that a handful of large employers have made public.

Commercial office vacancy rates will exceed 40 percent in major American cities by 2024, and the write-downs that follow will be the largest repricing of commercial property since the savings and loan crisis.

Landlords insist the market is cyclical. They said the same thing about retail in 2015, and about hotel occupancy in 2009, and both of those markets took the better part of a decade to recover the ground they lost.
